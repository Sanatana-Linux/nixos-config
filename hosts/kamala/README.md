### About the Goddess Kamala

Maa Kamala, also known as Kamalatmika, is the tenth and final Mahavidya, the wisdom goddess of the lotus. She is depicted seated upon a fully bloomed lotus, often flanked by two elephants anointing her with water from golden vessels — a scene that echoes Lakshmi, with whom she is closely associated. Her complexion is radiant and golden, and she is adorned in red and gold, holding two lotuses in her hands while the gesture of fearlessness and the gesture of granting boons grace the other two.

Kamala embodies prosperity, abundance, purity, and spiritual sovereignty. Her name means "she of the lotus," and the lotus itself is her central teaching: rooted in the mud yet rising unstained to bloom above the water, it represents a mind that lives fully in the world without being soiled by it. As the last of the Mahavidyas, she is understood to crown the journey through the previous nine — the peace and abundance that settle once the fierce work of the others is complete. Worshipped especially during Diwali, she is invoked for wealth, fertility, harmony, and the stable flourishing of a household or kingdom.
